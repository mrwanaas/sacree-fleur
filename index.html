Our Story

A little pearl
of Montmartre

Tucked away at 50 Rue Clignancourt, a five-minute stroll from Sacré-Cœur, Sacrée Fleur has been a neighbourhood institution since chef-owner Stéphane Ducreux opened its doors in 2010.

With only ten tables, every evening is an intimate affair. We source the finest cuts of Charolais beef, treat them with nothing but heat, salt, and time — then serve them sizzling on volcanic hot stone so you finish the cooking exactly as you like it.

There are no vegetarian options and no compromises. Only honest French cuisine, carefully chosen wines, and the kind of warmth that makes you want to come back.

Beyond Paris

From Montmartre
to Tokyo

What started as a small neighbourhood bistro at the foot of the Butte grew into something much larger. Stéphane Ducreux's vision of authentic French beef cookery proved as irresistible in Japan as it had in Paris.

2010 Sacrée Fleur opens at 50 Rue Clignancourt, Montmartre, Paris.
2017 First Tokyo location opens, in partnership with Spiceworks — operators of 100 Japanese establishments.
2019 A second Tokyo location follows in March. A third opens in Karuizawa, Nagano Prefecture, in April.

Seared in the kitchen, the stone arrives at your table still blazing with heat. You control the finish — rare, medium, well-done — cut by cut. The centrepiece of every Sacrée Fleur evening.

The Craft

Even the fries
are serious

"At Sacrée Fleur, we pay particular attention to every detail — right down to the frites."

Every week, 150 kilos of Agria potatoes arrive at the restaurant. They are peeled and hand-cut, then soaked in fresh water. A first fry at 160°C for 6 minutes cooks them through without browning. After resting for thirty minutes, the second fry at 180°C for 3 minutes delivers the crisp golden crust you'll remember.

This is what Montmartre cooking looks like: no shortcuts, even on the side dish.

— Stéphane Ducreux, Chef & Owner

What makes a perfect rib steak?

A beautiful piece of meat, properly marbled — neither too fatty nor too lean. Grilled carefully, with the flame adjusted just right. If you want it rare, it must be hot throughout. Bloody means very juicy. Medium means not overcooked. At Sacrée Fleur, we have always strived to serve you the very best.
